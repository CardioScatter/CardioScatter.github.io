Overview

CardioScatter maps differential cardiac voltage directly onto a frequency-modulated parametric response—removing the rectifier, DC rail, and powered electronics from the tag. The reader separates responses, compensates slow carrier drift, and reconstructs continuous ECG on a smartphone.

Tag and reader mechanism

Separate the pump. Preserve the ECG.

Three nested passive resonators divide sensing, regeneration, and radiative coupling. The reader then separates occupied bands and runs the same recovery branch for each detected tag.

VTR

Voltage-tunable resonator

Maps the signed differential electrode voltage to a resonance-frequency shift.

POR

Parametric oscillatory resonator

Transfers pump energy into a frequency-modulated response separated from the pump.

RER

Radiation-enhancing resonator

A larger outer loop increases the response’s magnetic dipole moment and strengthens coupling to the reader.

Implementation

Zero powered electronics on body. Real-time ECG on a smartphone.

The prototype combines a three-resonator tag with a MAX2871-based RF pump, separate transmit and receive antennas, an RTL-SDR Blog V3, and smartphone processing and display.

FAQ

The short answers.

Is the on-body tag battery-free?

Yes. The tag contains no battery, rectifier, DC rail, powered analog front end, ADC, or digital logic. The RF reader and smartphone are powered devices.

Where does signal processing run?

Tag detection, slow carrier-drift tracking, FM demodulation, tag-specific calibration, and ECG reconstruction run at the off-body reader and smartphone.

What has been demonstrated for multiple tags?

The prototype concurrently recovers two fully passive ECG streams with one RF source and reader—no separate excitation per tag. The representative eight-second segment reaches Pearson correlations of 0.956 and 0.949.

What operating range was evaluated?

CardioScatter reaches 30 cm with the portable 20 dBm reader. An externally powered reader-side 1 W amplifier extends ECG recovery to 1.2 m while the tag remains fully passive. At 1.2 m, R-peak F1 is 0.993, Pearson correlation is 0.889, and availability is 0.872. Human evaluations use 25 cm by default.
